About Me

I am a psychology student interested in understanding how people think, learn, remember, and interact with the world around them. My coursework has helped me connect psychology concepts to everyday experiences instead of seeing them as ideas that only belong in a textbook.

My Cognition Experience

Completing the cognition labs gave me a better understanding of how the mind processes information in ways that I normally do not think about. The labs on attention, perception, memory, and decision-making showed me that cognition is not always as simple or automatic as it feels.

This experience is valuable to me because I am studying psychology and want to better understand how people think and behave. Professionally, recognizing limits in attention and memory can help me be more patient and understanding when working with other people. Personally, the labs also made me more aware of how distractions and multitasking can affect my own performance.

Reflection: How My Thinking Changed

At the beginning of the course, I mostly thought of cognition as things like remembering information, paying attention, and solving problems. After completing the modules and labs, I see cognition as a much bigger process involving perception, attention, language, memory, decision-making, and social thinking.

I also realized that my own cognitive abilities are not fixed or perfect. Performance can change depending on distractions, stress, the amount of information I am processing, and how I approach a task. Since Module One, I have become more interested in ways to improve my cognitive skills, such as getting enough rest, reducing distractions, taking breaks, writing important information down, and being more intentional about technology.
